Introduction
A Note to the Reader

Why This Guide Exists

Thank you for taking the time to become a more informed voter. This guide is a straightforward look at the issues shaping House District 28 — and at the priorities I believe will help our community move forward.

Like many people across Southwest Portland, I have watched our community change over the years. Families are facing higher costs. Employers are navigating growing uncertainty. And many parents are asking a fair question: is our education system truly preparing students for success?

Those concerns are why I decided to run for State Representative. I do not believe the best answers begin in a conference room in Salem. They begin by listening to the people who live with the consequences of the decisions made there. This campaign is built around direct conversations, careful listening, and a commitment to represent the priorities of District 28 — not the demands of political insiders.

My background is not in politics. It is in preparation, service, and responsibility: more than two decades in medicine, earlier work in software, and service in the Army Medical Corps. Each taught me to understand complex systems, find where they break down, and focus on solutions that actually work in the real world. I intend to bring that same disciplined, evidence-led approach to the Oregon House.

The pages that follow explain the challenges facing our community, the priorities guiding this campaign, and why these issues matter for the future of District 28. I hope you will read it, share it, and reach out with your own thoughts.

Randall Fryer
Candidate for State Representative · Oregon House District 28
Looking Ahead: House District 2802
Part I
The Landscape

The Issues Shaping House District 28

Across District 28, residents are raising a consistent set of concerns. These are not abstract, partisan debates — they are practical questions about affordability, opportunity, and whether state government is delivering results for the money it spends.

01

Education & Schools

Parents are asking whether classrooms are focused on the fundamentals and whether students are graduating truly prepared for what comes next.

02

Taxes & the Cost of Living

Families and employers work hard for every dollar they earn — and are feeling the weight of rising taxes, fees, and everyday costs.

03

Business & the Economy

Local employers face growing uncertainty. A healthy economy depends on businesses that can invest, hire, and grow with confidence.

04

Public Safety

Communities want to be safe, with strong partnerships between residents and the law enforcement professionals who serve them.

05

Government Accountability

Oregonians deserve transparency and restraint — clear evidence that public programs deliver real value for the taxes they collect.

06

Opportunity for the Future

Every one of these issues shares a common thread: creating more opportunity for the families and generations who call District 28 home.

The chapters that follow lay out how Randall Fryer proposes to address these challenges — organized around three clear priorities and a set of guiding principles.

Looking Ahead: House District 2803
Priority 01
Priority One
01

Strengthen Educational Excellence

Education is the foundation of opportunity. Oregon's students deserve schools where learning comes first, teachers are supported, parents have a meaningful voice, and classrooms prepare students for success in college, careers, military service, and civic life.

Focus Areas

Strong Academic Standards

Support high expectations that prepare students to read, write, reason, solve problems, and graduate ready for life after high school.

Focus on Core Subjects

Prioritize reading, writing, mathematics, history, and civics so students develop the knowledge and critical-thinking skills they need to succeed.

Support Teachers

Ensure professional teachers have the authority, resources, and classroom environments needed to teach effectively.

Parent & Local Involvement

Encourage meaningful parental involvement and strengthen the role of locally elected school boards in education decisions.

Measure Success by Student Learning

Education policy should be evaluated by student achievement and long-term outcomes, not simply by the amount of money spent.

Priority 02
Priority Two
02

Lower Taxes for Families & Employers

Families and employers work hard for every dollar they earn. Government has a responsibility to spend taxpayer dollars wisely, avoid unnecessary costs, and remain accountable to the people it serves.

Focus Areas

Oppose Unnecessary Taxes & Fees

Advocate for policies that avoid placing additional financial burdens on Oregon families and businesses.

Responsible Budgeting

Carefully review state spending and prioritize programs that deliver meaningful value to taxpayers.

Transparency & Accountability

Support clear reporting so Oregonians understand how public funds are used and whether government programs are achieving their intended purpose.

Protect Essential Services

Support responsible budgeting that maintains essential public services while improving efficiency and reducing waste where possible.

Priority 03
Priority Three
03

Reduce Barriers to Business Growth

A healthy economy depends on businesses that can invest, hire, innovate, and grow. Oregon should encourage entrepreneurship while maintaining reasonable protections for workers, consumers, and the environment.

Focus Areas

Reduce Unnecessary Regulation

Support efforts to reduce unnecessary government micromanagement while preserving appropriate public safeguards.

Simpler Tax Compliance

Encourage policies that make Oregon's tax system easier for employers to understand and comply with.

Improve Permitting

Support more timely and predictable permitting so businesses can invest with greater certainty.

Encourage Investment

Promote policies that encourage entrepreneurship, attract investment, and help employers create better jobs throughout Oregon.

Support Local Employers

Recognize that small businesses and local employers are essential to thriving communities and economic opportunity.

Part II
What Guides the Work

Guiding Principles

Beyond the three priorities, every decision should reflect the values that guide responsible public service.

01 · Public Safety

Support policies that help keep Oregon communities safe while respecting individual rights and strengthening partnerships between local communities and law enforcement.

02 · Local Decision-Making

Communities understand their own needs. Parents, educators, local officials, and residents should have meaningful input into decisions that affect their schools and neighborhoods.

03 · Accountable Government

Government should be transparent, fiscally responsible, and accountable to taxpayers. Public service is a responsibility that requires careful stewardship of public resources.
